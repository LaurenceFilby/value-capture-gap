The Value Capture Gap

Every pay packet sits inside a band. The ceiling is the value you create. The floor is your best alternative. Where you land between them is a negotiation, and most people land far below the ceiling.

The idea

Pay is bounded on both sides

At any point in a career, compensation sits between two numbers. The upper bound is the marginal value you produce for the organisation. The lower bound is your reservation wage, the best offer you could walk to tomorrow. An employer will never pay more than the first for long, and you will never accept less than the second for long.

The space between is shared. Above your pay sits uncaptured value: what you create that the firm keeps. Below it sits your retention margin: the premium that stops you leaving. Where pay settles depends on how visible your contribution is, how easy you are to replace, how hard your value is to attribute, and how well you negotiate.

The evidence

The gap shows up everywhere you look

Labour's slice keeps shrinking

Workers' share of nonfarm business output was 65.5% in 1947 and hit 52.8% in Q2 2026, the lowest since records began. At the economy level, the band is drifting toward capital.

Innovators keep about 2 cents in the dollar

William Nordhaus estimated that innovators captured only about 2.2% of the total value their innovations created across the postwar US economy. The rest went to consumers and the wider economy. Strategy II in its purest form.

The floor moves when you do

Median annual wage growth, Atlanta Fed tracker. Job switchers out-earned stayers by 1.3 points in September 2026, and switching paid 8.4% at the 2022 peak. Testing your alternative is how the floor gets priced.

Pay concentrates at the top

US W-2 wages, 2022. The 0.13% of workers earning over $1M took 5.3% of all wages. The 227 people above $50M averaged about $100M each. The gap widens as the band rises, but so does capture.
